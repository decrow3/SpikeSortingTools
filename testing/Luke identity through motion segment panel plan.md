# Luke identity-through-motion multi-segment audit

## Status

Planned on 2026-08-31 as a shared, sorter-neutral benchmark for the KS2 native
tracking and selective-KS4 correction programs. Six non-overlapping 10-minute
segments are frozen from existing input and motion-estimator features. No sorter
outcome was used to select them.

## Primary question

> Is motion mainly causing missed spikes, or does the same neuron move far
> enough through waveform space that a static-template sorter loses its
> identity across time?

The audit tests whether waveform-state movement within a candidate neuron
approaches or exceeds its distance to competing neurons, and whether KS4 labels
form consecutive pieces of a continuous time-ordered trajectory.

## Candidate-family selection

Select candidate identities without using KS2 or selective-correction results.
Use the accepted unwarped KS4 full sort and existing reviewed events only to
form hypotheses.

For each segment, freeze:

- up to eight units stable in the first and last 120 s, with adequate spikes in
  both flanks;
- all already-nominated MUA/fragment families with enough events in the
  segment, including unit 389 and KIASORT families 46 and 82 where present;
- up to four nearby competing units per candidate, selected from stationary
  flank waveform similarity and spatial overlap; and
- negative-control units that remain excellent throughout the segment.

Selection must be saved before measuring within-episode waveform trajectories.
Do not require the same candidate units to appear in every segment; report both
per-segment and repeated-family results where available.

## Time-resolved raw-waveform states

Extract all templates from the same unwarped raw/rescue voltage path and clock.
Use frozen 10-s windows with 5-s steps as the primary resolution. Allow 20-s
windows only as a prespecified low-spike-count fallback, applied by a rule fixed
before waveform outcomes are viewed.

For every candidate and time window, save:

- spike count and temporal support;
- multichannel median and mean waveform;
- amplitude-normalized waveform;
- peak channel and energy-weighted depth;
- peak amplitude and spatial footprint;
- raw and shrinkage-stabilized covariance; and
- contributing KS4 labels and reviewed-event IDs.

Windows with insufficient spikes remain missing; do not interpolate waveform
states across them for the primary analysis.

## Primary geometry

Define one frozen feature representation from the relative-quiet segment and
apply it unchanged to every segment. Report results in raw flattened-waveform
space and in the frozen low-dimensional representation.

For candidate neuron `i` at time `t`, compute:

1. adjacent-time state distance `D(i,t,t+1)`;
2. distance from the first stable flank state;
3. pre-to-motion and pre-to-post cosine;
4. peak-channel/depth and amplitude change;
5. distance to the closest frozen competing identity at the same or nearest
   supported time; and
6. the ambiguity ratio

\[
A_{i,t} =
\frac{D(\text{candidate state}_{i,t},\ \text{candidate reference}_{i})}
     {D(\text{candidate state}_{i,t},\ \text{nearest competitor state}_{t})}.
\]

`A >= 1` means the state is at least as far from its own reference as from the
nearest competitor. Treat this as an ambiguity warning, not proof of a label
switch.

Distances must be uncertainty-normalized or compared with within-window
bootstrap variability so low-spike states do not appear artificially mobile.

## Time-ordered trajectory evidence

Represent candidate states as a directed graph ordered in time. An edge may
connect states only in adjacent supported time windows, with a separately
reported one-window-gap sensitivity analysis.

A KS4 fragment-family trajectory is supported when:

- adjacent states change smoothly relative to bootstrap variability;
- peak depth, amplitude, and waveform features follow a coherent path;
- successive KS4 labels occupy temporally complementary portions of that path;
- no competing stable unit supplies a shorter or equally plausible path;
- event-centered residual explanation improves when the states are treated as
  one time-varying family; and
- union refractory behavior is acceptable wherever fragments overlap in time.

Non-overlap alone supplies no CCG merge evidence. Do not treat the absence of
cross-fragment refractory violations as positive evidence when labels occupy
disjoint epochs.

## Segment-level endpoints

For every segment, report:

- reviewed-event detection fraction independent of unit label;
- fraction assigned to one stable identity, multiple fragments, MUA, or no
  event;
- median and 90th-percentile within-neuron state displacement;
- median and 90th-percentile ambiguity ratio;
- fraction of time windows with `A >= 1`;
- maximum adjacent-time discontinuity;
- pre-to-post return versus persistent state shift;
- number of defensible time-ordered fragment trajectories;
- nearest-neighbor identity changes; and
- dependence on motion magnitude, input anomaly, and support status.

The primary trend test is ordered across the three cleaner motion-gradient
segments. Analyze large motion with input anomaly as an interaction/replication
case. Do not pool it or the sustained-noise and support-dropout controls into
the clean trend.

## Shared benchmark for KS2

Run the same frozen segment panel through upstream KS2 after its installation
and batch-boundary gates pass. For each prespecified KS4 trajectory candidate,
ask whether KS2 returns:

- one unit spanning both stable flanks and the motion anchor;
- a continuous raw-waveform state path;
- acceptable refractory and duplicate behavior;
- greater event-centered residual explanation; and
- no merger with the frozen competing identities.

The primary KS2 benefit is a reduction in identity fragmentation that grows
with supported motion magnitude without a corresponding loss in the
relative-quiet or control segments.

## Shared benchmark for selective correction

If a selective KS4 operator later passes its confirmatory crossover and
switching gates, evaluate it on the identical panel. In addition to voltage
transition metrics, require template continuity across every identity-to-warp
and warp-to-identity boundary.

Selective correction helps only if it reduces the candidate's ambiguity ratio
and label fragmentation through high-motion epochs without manufacturing a new
waveform state at policy switches.

## Advancement logic

Evidence that identity continuity is a load-bearing failure mode requires:

- a graded increase in within-neuron waveform-state movement or fragmentation
  across the supported-motion panel;
- replication across multiple candidate families and at least two motion
  segments;
- absence of the same ordered pattern in the sustained-noise and support-
  dropout controls, or a clear explanation of their distinct failure mode;
- trajectories that are more plausible than paths through frozen competing
  units; and
- residual and refractory support rather than waveform cosine alone.

A single vivid family is a case study, not an advancement gate.

## Stop and interpretation rules

- If waveform-state movement is comparable across quiet, motion, and control
  segments, do not attribute KS4 fragmentation specifically to motion.
- If ambiguity rises only during support dropout or input anomaly, diagnose the
  input/support mechanism before testing a motion application.
- If candidate paths cross or approach competing-unit paths, label identity
  ambiguous; do not force a merge.
- If pre- and post-motion states are discontinuous with no supported
  intermediate path, the audit cannot establish same-neuron identity.
- Do not tune segment boundaries, waveform windows, feature axes, or competitors
  using KS2/selective-correction outcomes.
